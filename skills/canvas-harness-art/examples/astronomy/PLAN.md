# Astronomy: plan (written before any SVG)

Course name as lettered: **ASTRONOMY**. Course colour `#2F4A8C` (deep blue). Light theme. All three pieces.

Colours derived from the course colour
- key (62% course + 38% manila): `#76819E`. On the telescope tube and one scarf: things that could be any colour.
- chart blue `#34508E` (the course colour, barely lifted): the star chart the title is drawn on. A star chart really is deep blue.
- border ink: the course colour itself, `#2F4A8C` (7.6:1 on the sidebar).

## 1. What the course uses and does (the list)

1. Moon phases in order: new, waxing crescent, first quarter, waxing gibbous, full, waning gibbous, last quarter, waning crescent
2. Naked-eye observing: constellations joined star to star, a star chart, the ecliptic
3. Small refractor: tube, dew shield, objective lens, focuser, eyepiece
4. Finder scope on two stalks, aligned on a far target in daylight
5. Tripod with a yoke (alt-az) mount
6. Diffraction grating in a slide holder
7. A beam of starlight; the straight-through (zero order) spot
8. The first-order spectrum, violet deviated least, red most
9. Dark absorption lines: Ca H, G band, H-beta, Mg b, Na D, H-alpha (a Sun-like star)
10. A screen for the spectrum; a bench that carries holder and screen
11. Kepler I: ellipse with the Sun at one focus
12. Kepler II: equal areas in equal times; fast at perihelion, slow at aphelion
13. An orrery (wire orbit, Sun ball, planet bead)
14. H-R diagram: luminosity up, temperature falling to the right
15. Main sequence, giants, supergiants, white dwarfs; star colour follows temperature
16. Plotting one star (the Sun, G2) on the diagram

Set pieces chosen (5): **the moon-phase garland** (high, runs over two groups), **the refractor on its tripod** (long diagonal),
**the grating bench and spectrum screen** (low and long, then tall and narrow), **the Kepler orrery** (mid height, always turning),
**the H-R board** (tall, dark). Tall things: title chart, screen, H-R board, garland.

## 2. Main event (one sentence)

Starlight from the chart goes down the aligned telescope, crosses to the grating, fans into a spectrum with its dark lines on
the screen, and the star read from those lines is plotted on the H-R diagram.

## 5. Title

Three ideas
1. Letters as constellations (bright stars joined by lines) on a deep-blue star chart that stands on legs. *Chosen.*
2. Letters as dark absorption lines in one long spectrum strip. Rejected: black letters, six competing hues, illegible small.
3. Letters built from orbits and phases (O as an orbit, C-shapes as crescents). Rejected: only some letters can carry it; reads as decoration beside lettering.

Chosen: the chart is a whole board on two legs on the common floor line. Paper-coloured lines 5 wide join gold stars at every
corner of every letter; the telescope in the scene is aimed at the chart's brightest star, so the title is the thing being observed.
- deep blue = the night sky of the chart (and the user's course colour)
- paper = the chart's own constellation lines, neat line, ecliptic, crescent
- gold = stars / light (the same gold as the beam and the plotted star)
- one clay dab on the ecliptic = a planet (ties to the orrery's planet)
Living detail: the lines are retraced star to star, one letter at a time, late in the cycle; the target star flares when the tube is aimed. Then rest.

## 6. Overlaps (what crosses each gap, what is in front)

- title -> telescope: the dashed gold sight line from the target star to the objective; the garland's cord starts on the chart's corner.
- telescope -> bench: the gold beam; the garland overhead; the tripod's leg stands just before the bench's leg.
- bench -> screen: the fan of rays; the screen stands on the bench's end; the cord ends on the screen's corner.
- screen -> orrery -> H-R board: one floor, the orrery's wide hoop fills the gap at mid height.
- In front: figures over what they work on (F1 over the tube, F2 over the bench, F3 over the board); beam behind figures; slide over the beam; planet over hoop and post.

## 8. Border

Layout follows slots.md as it stands now: the icon stands bottom left, the text runs x 72-336.

- Ink: the course colour, deep indigo `#2F4A8C`. Paint: gold, with the key colour on the planet.
- Corner signature: a crescent Moon with one star, top left, above the icon.
- Top edge: a seven-star constellation zigzag joined on the pen line (its brightest star gold), one loose star, a short hour scale.
- Right edge: a spectrum's absorption lines as short dashes of different weights.
- Bottom left: a plain floor line for the icon.
- Bottom edge, right of the icon: a long flat orbit with a gold Sun at its focus.
- Traveller: a planet on that orbit, three quarters of the bottom edge and back every 12 s, whipping past the Sun and slow
  at the far end (Kepler's equation, e = 0.6). It passes behind the Sun on the far side and in front on the near side.

## 10. Hand-over note

**Banner.** A star chart on legs carries the name. A garland of the eight Moon phases runs from it over a refractor on a
tripod and a bench with a diffraction grating to a screen. Past the screen a Kepler orrery turns, and at the right a
student plots a star on an H-R diagram.
**What moves (16 s).** The tube is nudged onto the chart's bright star; a beam leaves the eyepiece; the grating slide is
lowered into it; six rays fan out and the spectrum with its absorption lines spreads on the screen; the star is chalked
onto the main sequence. The orrery's planet never stops (8 s, quick near the Sun). Late in the loop the title's
constellation lines are retraced letter by letter.
**Title colours.** Deep blue = the night sky of the chart (your course colour). Paper lines = the chart's constellation
lines. Gold = stars and light, the same gold as the beam and the plotted star. The clay dot on the dashed ecliptic = a planet.
**Upload.** Customize workspace -> Course artwork -> choose Astronomy -> Upload or Paste SVG for banner, icon and border.
Banner size Large or Standard, fit contain, position right.
**Unsure of / may want changed.**
- The spectrum is a Sun-like (G) star's: Ca H, G band, H-beta, Mg b, Na D, H-alpha, placed by the grating equation for
  800 lines/mm. If your lab uses a different star or lamp, say which and the lines move.
- Moon phases are drawn as seen from the northern hemisphere (waxing lit on the right).
- Alternatives: (a) the orrery could become two students drawing an ellipse with two pins and a loop of string;
  (b) the icon could be the orrery or a crescent instead of the telescope.
