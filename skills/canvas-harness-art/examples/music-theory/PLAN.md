## 1. What the course uses and does

1. The grand staff: treble and bass staves joined by a brace
2. Treble (G) clef and bass (F) clef
3. Key signatures: sharps and flats in fixed order and position (F sharp first)
4. Intervals: thirds and fifths, stacked
5. Triads in root position (three notes on three neighbouring lines)
6. The circle of fifths: C at the top, sharps clockwise, flats anticlockwise
7. Four-part harmony, SATB, played keyboard style (three notes in the right hand, bass in the left)
8. A piano with its bench, pedals and a score on the desk
9. A metronome: pyramid case, pendulum rod, sliding brass weight, tempo scale, winding key
10. Conducting patterns: the four-beat pattern (down, left, right, up)
11. A baton, a conductor's stand and podium
12. A tuning fork, struck and held to the ear
13. Singing a pitch back (ear training)
14. A staff-lined chalkboard and chalk
15. Whole notes, stems, beams, bar lines, the final double bar
16. Manuscript paper

## 3. Main event (one sentence)

The key moves up a fifth: the hand on the circle is drawn from C to G, so one sharp (F sharp) is chalked into the key signature of both staves, the fork gives the pitch, the conductor beats one bar of four, and on the downbeat the pianist plays the new tonic chord and the singer sings it, all in time with the metronome.

Left to right: wheel -> board -> piano -> singer -> conductor -> metronome.

## 4. Cast (five figures, one accessory each)

| Who | Where | Goal -> action -> visible result -> reset |
|---|---|---|
| Turner (cap) | right of the wheel, facing it, holding a hooked pointer (revised: first stood in front of the wheel pushing the hand, which hid the hub and the hand behind the arm) | change the key -> leans in, hooks the eye of the hand and draws it from 12 to 1 o'clock, straightening up -> the hand points at G -> at the start of the loop pushes it back to C |
| Writer (bun) | in front of the board's empty bar, facing left | fix the key signature -> chalks the four strokes of a sharp on the top line of the treble staff, then on the fourth line of the bass staff -> two gold sharps -> rubs them out with a sweep of the hand at the start of the loop |
| Pianist (ponytail) | on the bench at the keyboard end, facing right | sound the tonic -> lifts both hands, drops them on the downbeat, nods -> sound leaves the open lid -> lifts the hands again |
| Singer (plum scarf) | in the crook of the piano, facing the conductor | find the pitch -> offers a palm, strikes the fork on it, raises the fork to the ear, sings on the downbeat -> the fork rings, three arcs leave the head -> lowers the fork |
| Conductor (tuft) | on the podium behind the stand, facing left | hold everyone together -> a small breath upward, then down / left / right / up -> the plum four-beat pattern is traced by the baton tip and the numbers 1-4 land with the beats -> holds the baton at the top |

The metronome's pendulum never stops: 2 s per full swing, a tick every second, and every beat of the bar lands on a tick.

## 6. Title

Three ideas:

1. Letters bent out of five parallel staff lines. Rejected: five thin lines per stroke turn to mush at 80 px.
2. Letters as black keys on a strip of keyboard. Rejected: reads as a font on a pattern, and says "piano", not "theory".
3. **Chosen: the name is engraved as the music on a grand staff.** A sheet of manuscript on a flip-chart easel carries a real grand staff (brace, treble clef curling round the G line, bass clef with its dots either side of the F line, final double bar). MUSIC stands on the treble staff and THEORY on the bass staff, each letter exactly one staff tall. The letters are written the way notes are engraved: thin stems, thick sloping beams and slurs. The O is a whole note with its slanted hole, the Y is a tuning fork.

Revised after the first render: the I was a quarter note (stem with a head in the first space). At Standard and Compact size it read as a J ("MUSJC"), so it became a stem between two beams.

Colours: plum = the notes of the name (the course colour: music ink can honestly be any colour); deep plum = the pen edge; ink = clefs, staff lines, bar lines; slate = the steel of the fork; paper = the manuscript sheet; kraft = the easel.

Living detail: the fork-Y rings (arcs grow either side) at the moment the singer strikes her fork, in sympathy; then rest.

## 7. Overlaps

- Title -> wheel: easel leg and A-frame leg share floor and shadow; the ground runs behind and past the sheet.
- Wheel -> board: the turner stands in the gap, the pointer reaches across the rim between the D and A labels to the hand.
- Board -> piano: the writer stands in front of the board's empty bar; the pianist's back and bench sit just past the board's right leg.
- Piano -> singer: she stands in the crook at the tail; the piano's sound arcs rise beside her.
- Singer -> conductor: her voice arcs, the stand, and the beat pattern hanging over the stand.
- Conductor -> metronome: the pendulum swings out toward his back.
- In front, always: figures over objects; raised arms over heads.

## 8. Icon

One thing: a **metronome** (key-colour case, ink rod, brass weight) standing on a ground stroke at the bottom of the box. One action: the rod ticks across and back twice, then rests tilted for 2 s. 5 s cycle, one moving part.

## 9. Border (revised to the row layout the skill now documents: icon bottom-left, text from x 72, count top-right)

- Ink: deep plum `#55294C` (about 10:1 on the sidebar); paint: gold; the key colour on the travelling note.
- Top edge: the pen line is a beam with six notes hanging from it, three bar ticks and one beamed pair.
- Top-left corner above the icon: a treble clef, the corner signature.
- Bottom strip, right of the icon: sixteen piano keys with their black keys, then a crescendo hairpin. The bottom-left is a plain floor line for the icon.
- Right side, below the count: a tuning fork with a gold wash.
- Traveller: a plum note slides along the top beam through 170 of the 360 units, rests, returns. When it arrives the fork rings. 12 s cycle.

# Hand-over note

**Files:** `music-theory-banner.svg` (1680 x 240), `music-theory-icon.svg` (100 x 100), `music-theory-border.svg` (360 x 110).

**The banner shows** the class moving a key up a fifth. From the left: the name on a grand staff; the circle of fifths with its hand being drawn from C to G; a chalkboard where the F sharp of the new key signature is written on both staves beside a G major chord; a grand piano; a singer taking her pitch from a tuning fork; a conductor beating four; a metronome.

**What moves** (16 s loop, mostly at rest): the wheel's hand and the turner's pointer; the writer's arm and the eight chalk strokes of the two sharps; the pianist's hands and head on the downbeat; the singer's two arms, the fork's ring and her voice; the conductor's arm, the traced pattern and its beat numbers; the metronome's pendulum (the only thing that never stops); the fork-Y in the title rings once.

**Title colours:** plum = the notes of the name (your course colour); dark plum = the pen edge; black = clefs, staff and bar lines; grey-blue = the steel tuning fork; white = manuscript paper.

**Upload:** Customize workspace -> Course artwork -> choose Music Theory -> Upload or Paste SVG for each slot. Banner size "Large" or "Standard", fit "contain", position "right".

**Things I was unsure of, and two alternatives:**

- The chord on the board is G major in keyboard style (G B D over a bass G) as whole notes with no time signature, as in a harmony workbook. If your class writes strict SATB on two staves with stems, I can redraw it that way.
- The I of MUSIC could go back to being a quarter note if you prefer the pun over the cleaner letter.
- The piano is a grand because it reads at 140 px; if your room has an upright and that matters, say so.
